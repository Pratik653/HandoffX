# HandoffX literature review — executed evidence-based scoping audit

**Audit date:** 28 October 2026. **Scope:** 28 original studies/benchmarks plus one secondary survey (29 records). **Status:** targeted, structured scoping review; **NOT** a comprehensive systematic review. **Peer review:** no independent external peer review of this report. **New empirical experiments:** none. **Mathematical development:** equivalence mapping only, not new HandoffX theory.

## Executive finding

The broad formulation “choose continue/retry/human/stop under uncertain probabilities” is a **finite-action sequential stochastic control problem**, with human assistance representable as an action-dependent transition or observation kernel. MDP/POMDP and robust-control frameworks already cover such choices **conditionally on a state representation, objective, transition uncertainty sets and observability assumptions**. A universally reliable rule without identifiability or distributional assumptions is not implied by these theories.

Static machine-versus-human allocation, estimation of expert correctness, limited human labels, multi-expert routing, and cost-sensitive loss are established (Madras et al., 2018; Raghu et al., 2019; Mozannar & Sontag, 2020; Verma & Nalisnick, 2022; Verma et al., 2023). Dynamic switching is already explicitly formulated as a two-layer MDP (Balazadeh Meresht et al., 2022). Recent work advances LLM model escalation (Fanconi & van der Schaar, 2025; Shaikh, 2026), human-intervention anticipation (Huq et al., 2026), and practical help-seeking evaluation (HiL-Bench, 2026). See `HANDOFFX_PRIOR_ART_MATRIX.csv` for study-by-study traceability.

**Decision:** Phase 1 gate **C (provisionally)**: focus on comparative empirical and software/reproducibility value, with a possible bounded algorithmic contribution if its performance survives strong baselines; **not** a claim that the Bellman equation or a new handoff threshold is novel.

## 1. Problem and research questions

Main question: Can a model-agnostic parameterized controller choose **continue, retry, human assistance or stop**, accounting for uncertainty in both AI recovery and human assistance effectiveness? Model-agnostic means no specific LLM architecture is assumed, **not** that parameters or outcomes transfer unchanged. Seven subquestions: (RQ1) structural transfer; (RQ2) timing; (RQ3) joint probability uncertainty; (RQ4) model/operator transfer; (RQ5) reliability and limits; (RQ6) low-compute implementation; (RQ7) comparative results.

In the evidence map below, **E** means established mathematical or empirical formulation in the cited scope, **P** means partial evidence, **U** means unverified specific LLM-agent gap rather than a mathematical impossibility:

| RQ | Existing evidence | Verdict for HandoffX |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1 model-agnostic decision rule | General MDP/POMDP, learning-to-switch MDP, robust controllers | **E** structural abstraction; **U** empirical transfer across LLM tool agents |
| RQ2 when to continue/defer/stop | Selective prediction; Bayes deferral; optimal stopping; within-generation escalation | **E** conditional optimal policies; **P** four-action tool workflows |
| RQ3 joint uncertainty | Robust MDPs model arbitrary action-transition uncertainty; calibrated expert models and uncertainty-aware help seeking exist | **E** abstract representation; **P** jointly learned AI-retry and human-resolution calibration |
| RQ4 across model/operator changes | ML calibration, OpenL2D expert variability, multi-team MDP | **E** need for conditional modeling; **U** validated adapter recalibration and failure diagnostics across LLMs |
| RQ5 guarantees | Robust DP under rectangularity, L2D consistency, selected escalation regret bounds, conformal risk control | **E** qualified guarantees; **U** guarantees without unverifiable assumptions on logged outcomes |
| RQ6 lightweight library | Existing methods and benchmarks are separable; no claim of an exhaustive software ecosystem search | **P** potential research-grade integration; uniqueness of library **unverified** |
| RQ7 comparisons | OpenL2D, FiFAR, τ-bench, HiL-Bench, cascades and other experiments | **E** relevant benchmarks; **U** controlled four-action joint-uncertainty comparisons |

## 2. Strongest mathematical comparators — method, assumptions, evidence

### 2.1 Mozannar and Sontag (2020): static expert deferral

**Source and inspected scope:** original ICML PDF on PMLR, specifically objective and Bayes rule on pp. 2–4; experimental tables and discussion pp. 6–8. The original system loss, Eq. (1), has classifier loss on nondeferred cases plus expert loss on deferred cases. With zero-one costs, Eq. (5) gives the pointwise Bayes reject rule: defer when the predicted expert correctness is at least the model's largest class posterior. The experts are stochastic, not assumed perfectly correct. The paper supplies a cost-sensitive reduction and a consistent surrogate loss. It evaluates CIFAR10H human labels and image/text tasks; its CheXpert expert is constructed synthetically. **HandoffX overlap:** essentially the static human-versus-AI decision; estimating both sides is not new. **Boundary:** no sequential retry kernel, human queue, or dynamic stop terminal. [Primary](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/mozannar20b.html), [inspected PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/mozannar20b/mozannar20b.pdf).

### 2.2 Verma and Nalisnick (2022); Verma et al. (2023): human competence calibration

The 2022 paper identifies lack of expert-correctness calibration in an earlier softmax L2D method and proposes one-vs-all estimation of calibrated expert correctness while retaining consistency. The 2023 AISTATS paper considers multiple experts, explicit `P(m_j=y|x)`, consistency, calibration propagation and conformal expert subsets. **Overlap:** a statement that HandoffX uniquely recognizes uncertainty in human effectiveness is false. **Boundary:** abstracts do not establish simultaneous sequential AI recovery, dependent retries, and human intervention outcomes. Source level: official PMLR abstract, not full paper proof audit. [2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/verma22c.html); [2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/verma23a.html).

### 2.3 Iyengar (2005), Nilim and El Ghaoui (2005): robust stochastic control

A state-dependent action model can put `continue`, `retry`, `human`, `stop` in the same finite action set; each action has its own cost and transition law. Robust MDPs choose a policy against an ambiguity set of transition laws; Iyengar stresses *rectangularity* conditions for robust dynamic programming. Nilim and El Ghaoui derive robust dynamic-programming approaches for uncertain transition matrices and demonstrate path-planning robustness. **Overlap:** uncertainty about both AI and human outcome transition probabilities is subsumed if both are entries of the uncertain transition kernel. **Caveat:** simultaneous/correlated parameter uncertainty may be *non-rectangular*; naïvely applying state-action minimax recursions could give wrong guarantees. Source level: original publisher metadata/abstract, not full proof re-derivation. [Iyengar](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.1040.0129); [Nilim & El Ghaoui](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.1050.0216).

### 2.4 Suilen et al. (2020); Cubuktepe et al. (2021): robust partial observability

uPOMDP work includes uncertain state transitions and uncertain observations, robust specification satisfaction and finite-state policies. Cubuktepe et al. describe semi-infinite optimization, dualization and local linear-program approximations, evaluated on collision avoidance and spacecraft planning. **Overlap:** partial observability + uncertain human and machine actions are *not* enough to claim mathematical novelty; these are labels on action kernels. **Limit:** the existing studies do not directly empirically validate human-assisted LLM tool-agent failure recovery. Source level: official IJCAI and AAAI abstracts. [IJCAI](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2020/569); [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/17401).

### 2.5 Balazadeh Meresht et al. (2022): online switching among agents

**Particularly important.** This TMLR paper specifies a *two-layer MDP* for switching control among team agents; its algorithm uses upper confidence bounds on agent policies and environment transitions and reports sublinear switching-policy regret. It also uses shared bounds across teams in similar environments and obstacle avoidance simulations. **Overlap:** sequential control switching with estimated effectiveness and regret is established. **Limit:** the original PDF abstract and publisher announcement were accessible, but the OpenReview web interface was challenged. We cannot independently certify the entire proof or transfer result from the abstract alone. [Publisher entry](https://openreview.net/forum?id=NT9zgedd3I); [accessible original PDF](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=NT9zgedd3I).

### 2.6 Rosenthal et al. (2011); Singi et al. (2023); Fuchs et al. (2023): imperfect humans and assistance

Rosenthal et al.'s HOP-POMDP learns human accuracy and availability online. Singi et al. use uncertainty in expected return to request expert assistance under limited calls and full/partial observations. Fuchs et al. simulate environment-dependent sensing failures for both human and AI and train an RL delegation manager; their published methods emphasize delegation/switching penalty, not all intervention operating costs. **Overlap:** people are known to be fallible or unavailable; limited human budgets and repeated decisions are not HandoffX inventions. **Boundary:** no inspected work specifically demonstrates a validated four-action tool-agent policy under jointly miscalibrated recovery/rescue and correlation across retries. [HOP-POMDP](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/7980); [Singi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06710); [Fuchs](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/7/3409).

### 2.7 Ghoummaid and Shalit (2024): selective labels and confounding

This NeurIPS paper learns deferral from observational decisions using causal costs robust to bounded hidden confounding; reports synthetic and semisynthetic experiments. **Overlap:** the data collection/identification problem is already a research topic. **Boundary:** cannot assume those assumptions solve counterfactual rescue outcomes for arbitrary tool agents. [NeurIPS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/660cf2a1eabe448920a3ab6754555adb-Abstract-Conference.html).

### 2.8 Shaikh (2026): direct LLM-agent stopping competitor

**Source:** original arXiv HTML manuscript read in detail, including assumptions, recursion, threshold proof and simulation/real-model sections; **preprint, not peer-reviewed verification**. It models a junior model's eventual success posterior `B_t = P(Y=1 | F_t)`, conditionally independent evidence given the eventual outcome, fixed senior-model success `q`, and escalation cost `gamma`; one action continues, the other escalates to a stronger AI. It asserts a time-dependent optimal threshold, a calibration-regret bound and `O(n^-1/2)` plug-in regret. Simulation includes an AR(1) dependent-signal misspecification stress test. Its version 1.1 tests a **constant threshold**, not the full dynamic-program policy, on **257 MBPP tasks using one 1.5B→7B Qwen code cascade**, and acknowledges that limited setting. **Overlap:** newness of optimal stopping, threshold rules, and calibration-regret in LLM self-escalation is specifically contradicted. **Boundary:** no uncertain human expert q, no separate retry action, no actual human queue or operator heterogeneity; one real-model pair does not establish broad transfer. [Primary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24087).

### 2.9 Fanconi and van der Schaar (2025): cascaded LLMs plus humans

Their NeurIPS framework chooses between base-model answer, stronger LLM regeneration, and ultimately human escalation, with online human-feedback adaptation. It reports evaluation on general and medical QA datasets. **Overlap:** sequential model routing, retry-like regeneration, and human escalation already coexist; do **not** claim that merely having those actions is new. **Boundary:** the accessible abstract does not support a claim of joint epistemic recovery/rescue robustness or correlated tool retries. [NeurIPS](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/10e0c427408ccc6e073d9464e2280f89-Abstract-Conference.html).

### 2.10 Alves et al. (2025): expert capacity and realistic benchmarking

The peer-reviewed Scientific Data study supplies OpenL2D and FiFAR: **30,000 instances and 50 synthetic fraud analysts**, with expert workload constraints and realistic single-expert-label training scenarios. The authors explicitly show expert composition changes baseline rankings; they compare synthetic patterns with real analyst decision behavior but **do not** thereby turn synthetic expert outcomes into actual counterfactual human outcomes. **Overlap:** synthetic expert benchmarking, heterogeneous expertise and capacities already exist. **Boundary:** per-case classification, not multi-step tool failure/recovery, and no both-side posterior shift test. [Primary full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04664-y).

### 2.11 Huq et al. (2026); HiL-Bench (2026); τ-bench (2025); τ²-bench (2026)

Huq et al.'s **final ACL Anthology record** reports 400 real-user web navigation trajectories, over 4,200 interleaved actions and a user study. Importantly the final publication reports **36.8%** increased user-rated usefulness, whereas an older arXiv version reports **26.5%**; these are *version-dependent figures*, not interchangeable. HiL-Bench tests if coding and SQL agents ask for targeted clarification when blockers appear progressively. τ-bench measures tool-agent-user interactions and `pass^k`; τ²-bench introduces a dual-control Dec-POMDP telecom environment where both user and agent act with tools. **Overlap:** asking at the right time, sequential observation, and interactive agent evaluation already have primary work. **Boundary:** predicting user desire to intervene ≠ optimally requesting a human with uncertain repair success. [ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.2029/); [HiL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09408); [τ-bench](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/1b126cc38b8638e07bef37e7b2bb72bf-Abstract-Conference.html); [τ²](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/barres26a.html).

### 2.12 Further boundaries: Jitkrittum (2023), Cortes et al. (2026), Angelopoulos et al. (2024)

Jitkrittum et al. characterize when confidence-based deferral fails owing to specialist downstream models, label noise and distribution shift. Cortes et al. (ICML 2026) introduce margin-based imbalanced cost-sensitive deferral with LLM routing experiments. Conformal risk control has finite-sample guarantees for specified monotone losses under sampling conditions; it is **not** a universal guarantee for nonmonotone four-action agent policies. [Jitkrittum](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/1f09e1ee5035a4c3fe38a5681cae5815-Abstract-Conference.html); [Cortes](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/cortes26b.html); [Conformal](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/f3549ef9b5ff520a7e41ff3cc306ab2b-Abstract-Conference.html).

## 3. Formal equivalence mapping (established theory, **not** a new derivation)

Let the controller's decision state `z_t` contain task progress, evidence/history summary, budget and (optionally) a belief about latent fault modes and human availability. Let its actions be `A={continue,retry,human,stop}`; provide an action-specific immediate cost `c(z,a)`, state transition `P_a(z'|z)` and observation kernel if hidden. Stopping transitions to a terminal failure/abandonment state; human assistance transitions to resolution, delay, unavailability or failed help. Then, **under a Markov and known-parameter model**, the standard finite-horizon Bellman principle chooses `argmin_a{c + E[V_{t+1}]}`. If probabilities or observations are set-valued, robust MDP/uPOMDP frameworks apply subject to their admissibility and solution assumptions. If retry correlation is caused by latent persistent faults, augment the state/belief with the fault mode: the correlation is then representable without inventing a new equation. This is a **representation argument**, not a verified equivalence between any particular fitted HandoffX estimator and all prior algorithms.

The complementary one-step model is also existing expected-loss minimization: for machine success `p_A`, human success `p_H`, common failure cost `L`, and handoff overhead `c_H`, choose human if `c_H+(1-p_H)L < (1-p_A)L`, or equivalently `p_H-p_A > c_H/L` when `L>0`. This is an illustrative special case, **not** a novel result; it ignores retries, waits, differing failure severities and information value.

**Counterexample to universal rule:** two environments with identical observed estimates and costs but swapped true success rates for machine and human produce opposite optimal actions. Without informative evidence or assumptions limiting this ambiguity, no policy based only on those observations can be optimal in both. **Counterexample to simple threshold:** finite human wait time, deadlines and persistent recoverable faults can yield different actions at equal instantaneous AI confidence; policy depends on budget, time and fault beliefs, not confidence alone.

## 4. Explicit solved / partially solved / unresolved map

**Already solved mathematically (conditional):** static loss-based deferral; POMDP modeling; robust DP under stated uncertainty structure; switching between policies; calibrated expert prediction; ordinary optimal stopping; certain risk control and regret guarantees. **Partially solved empirically:** human availability and fallibility in robotics; multi-expert capacities; agent help-seeking and model cascades; transfer across some data/task domains. **Not established by this targeted evidence audit:** a benchmark and calibrated, auditable policy comparison covering all four **tool-agent** actions with **simultaneously imperfect AI-retry and human-resolution estimates**, latent correlated retries, missing outcome labels, shift/recalibration, and strong robust/control/defer baselines under **equal information**. This is a **candidate conjunction-specific empirical gap, not a proof of absence of earlier work**.

## 5. Seven-RQ synthesis

- **RQ1:** Mathematical structure is model-agnostic, but admissible state/action adapters must expose outcomes and restrictions. The research novelty is not the parameter interface itself.
- **RQ2:** A policy can be optimized with known transition models; Shaikh, Jitkrittum and classical theory remove novelty in threshold discovery. Whether the four actions are estimable in an actual tool agent is separate.
- **RQ3:** Robust ambiguity about all action kernels is representable; empirical reliability of *estimated joint* uncertainty under limited counterfactuals remains weakly evidenced here.
- **RQ4:** Structural transfer may hold while calibration changes; the corpus does not justify unchanged decision performance after changing LLMs, operators or tools.
- **RQ5:** Guarantees require assumptions about ambiguity-set coverage, identifiability, horizon, stationarity and rectangularity. Real-world safety is not proved by model-based optimization.
- **RQ6:** A CPU-only adapter-free policy evaluation and simulator library is plausible; uniqueness requires a separate software ecosystem inventory.
- **RQ7:** No HandoffX-specific experiments have been run, so there is **no measured HandoffX gain**. The correct empirical null includes simple policies performing as well as sophisticated ones.

## 6. Quality and limitation summary

Evidence strength differs: original full manuscript text inspected for Mozannar–Sontag and Shaikh; full publisher HTML for Alves and Fuchs; most remaining papers assessed via primary official abstract/metadata only. Mathematical guarantees mentioned for those papers are **authors' stated claims**, not independently checked proofs. No formal multiple-reviewer screening, exhaustive database search, DOI resolution census, reproducible search hit counts, full-text pass for all 29 records or PRISMA accounting has occurred. The novelty verdict is therefore **defensible provisional scoping judgment**, not a published systematic-review finding.
